# Product-name autofill on first scan is a dismissible suggestion, not automatic infill, sourced live from Open Food Facts then UPCitemdb with a barcode-keyed shared cache

When an unregistered barcode is scanned during batch add ([Batch stock entry with barcode scanning](https://linear.app/per-enstrom/issue/PER-274)), inline registration opens a form to pick or create the pantry item. We decided this form should try, automatically and silently, to suggest a product name via a public lookup — but never write into the name field on its own. The field always starts blank and stays under manual control; a separate suggestion slot below it shows a tappable chip with the found name once/if a lookup resolves, and tapping the chip copies it into the field, overwriting whatever's there. The suggestion still appears even if the household has already started typing before it lands — it's inert until tapped, so a late arrival can't clobber anything. This split (rather than autofilling the field directly) keeps the household in control of what gets submitted while still saving typing on the common case, and sidesteps a field that silently rewrites itself out from under a household mid-type.

The lookup chain for v1 is Open Food Facts first, then UPCitemdb's free tier as fallback — each tried with a ~2s timeout, so ~4s worst case. Open Food Facts' sister databases (Open Beauty/Pet Food/Products Facts), which the originating research ([PER-277](https://linear.app/per-enstrom/issue/PER-277)) recommended chaining in, are deliberately deferred: three extra calls per unregistered scan buys little for a pantry/grocery-focused app and can be added later if real coverage gaps show up. A "match" requires a non-empty product name; Open Food Facts' `status:1`-but-empty-name case is treated identically to its `status:0` miss — there's nothing to suggest either way. Only the name is used — Hyllan's pantry item has no brand field (see CONTEXT.md), and a match's brand is dropped rather than folded into the name, keeping the suggested value exactly what would land in that single field. While a lookup is in flight the suggestion slot shows a brief "Looking up product…" placeholder, clearing to empty on a miss or timeout with no error surfaced — a miss is a normal, silent outcome, not a failure state.

Lookup results are cached keyed on the canonical barcode value (the same UPC-A→EAN-13 normalization as [ADR 0007](0007-barcode-registration-domain-model.md)), shared across all households rather than scoped to one. A barcode's product name is a property of the physical product, not of any household that happens to scan it, so the same lookup would otherwise be repeated by every household that scans a common product — a waste against Open Food Facts' 15 req/min/IP limit that only gets worse as the household count grows. The cache is the main lever against that ceiling. Open Food Facts' ODbL license requires a "via Open Food Facts" credit wherever its data is surfaced; that text sits directly beside the suggestion chip, appearing and disappearing with it, since that's the only place this data is shown.

## Considered Options

- Autofilling the name field directly on a match — rejected; a field that rewrites itself after the household has started typing is surprising and risks clobbering an in-progress correction. A separate, tap-to-accept suggestion keeps the field under manual control at all times.
- Chaining all of Open Food Facts' sister databases (Beauty/Pet Food/Products Facts) in v1, as the originating research suggested — rejected for now; added latency and complexity for a thin hit-rate gain given Hyllan's grocery/pantry focus. Deferred, not ruled out permanently.
- Folding a match's brand into the suggested name (e.g. "Barilla Fusilli") — rejected; Hyllan's pantry item has no brand field, so the suggestion should be exactly what the name field would hold, nothing appended.
- Household-scoped lookup caching (or no caching at all) — rejected; the barcode-to-name mapping is a property of the product, not the household, so a shared cache avoids redundant calls against a shared, tight rate limit.
- Suppressing a late-arriving suggestion once the household has started typing — rejected; the chip is inert until tapped, so showing it late costs nothing and may still be useful.
